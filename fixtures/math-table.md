# Speed, distance and time

When something moves, we can ask how fast it goes. Speed tells us how much distance is covered in each unit of time. If we write $v$ for speed, $d$ for distance and $t$ for time, the rule is $v = d/t$.

The same rule written as a fraction:

$$v = \frac{d}{t}$$

So a cyclist who rides 30 kilometres in 2 hours has a speed of $30 / 2 = 15$ km/h. If you know the speed and the time, you can find the distance with $d = v \times t$, and if you know the distance and the speed, the time is $t = d / v$.

Units matter. Distance in kilometres and time in hours give a speed in km/h. Distance in metres and time in seconds give a speed in m/s. Never mix units without converting first: 1 km/h is about 0.28 m/s.

## Example trips

| Traveller             | Distance covered (km) | Time taken (hours) | Average speed (km/h) | Means of transport and remarks |
| --------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------ | -------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Walking student       | 4                     | 1                  | 4                    | On foot, flat road to school   |
| Cyclist               | 30                    | 2                  | 15                   | Bicycle, with one short stop   |
| School bus            | 45                    | 1.5                | 30                   | City traffic, many bus stops   |
| Family car on highway | 240                   | 3                  | 80                   | Motorway, speed limit 90 km/h  |
| High-speed train      | 600                   | 2                  | 300                  | Electric train, no stops       |

Notice that the train is 75 times faster than the walking student: $300 / 4 = 75$.
