# Photosynthesis: how plants make their food

## What plants need

Green plants make their own food. They do not eat like animals do. Instead, they use three simple ingredients: light from the Sun, water from the soil and carbon dioxide from the air. The water travels up from the roots through the stem to the leaves. The carbon dioxide enters the leaves through tiny openings called stomata.

## Where it happens

Photosynthesis happens mostly in the leaves, inside small green parts of the cells called chloroplasts. Chloroplasts contain a green pigment called chlorophyll. Chlorophyll absorbs sunlight, especially red and blue light, and reflects green light. That is why most leaves look green to our eyes.

## What plants make

Using the energy of the light, the plant joins water and carbon dioxide to make a sugar called glucose. Glucose is the plant's food: it gives the plant energy to grow, and it can be stored as starch in roots, seeds and fruits. Oxygen is made at the same time. The plant releases most of this oxygen into the air through the stomata, and animals, including humans, breathe it in.

A short way to write the process is: carbon dioxide + water, with light, gives glucose + oxygen.

## Why it matters

Almost every food chain on Earth begins with photosynthesis. Animals that eat plants get the energy the plants stored, and animals that eat other animals get it in turn. Photosynthesis also keeps the air breathable, because it removes carbon dioxide and adds oxygen. Forests, grasslands and tiny algae in the ocean all take part.
